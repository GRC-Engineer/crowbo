# Pixel Archivist and topology feathers

Created 24 September 2026 with the built-in image generation tool. These are concept boards for review, not selected production assets. The homepage and approved mascot are unchanged.

## Deliverables

- [Two Archivist studies](archivist-pixel-studies-v1.png): Terminal Archivist uses larger pixel clusters; Feather Archivist retains more layered plumage.
- [Six topology feathers](topology-feather-studies-v2.png): Spine, Branch, Mesh, Loop, Braid and Cluster vary the feather anatomy as well as the embedded paths.

The proposed direction is the chunkier Terminal Archivist, with a family of feather silhouettes that can distinguish parts of the network. The names describe visual metaphors; they do not define product architecture or evidence weights. Actual homepage-size legibility and motion still need to be checked after a direction is chosen.

### Feather comparison, initial generation

Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: six-part Crowbo topology-feather concept board.
Primary request: Make SIX distinct feather shapes whose actual anatomy and silhouettes embody SIX different network topologies. Detailed pixel-art feathers for the same identity as the reference crow. The shape IS the network, not six identical feathers with different circuit badges printed on them.
Input image: approved Crowbo Archivist identity reference for purple/mint/lemon palette, angular plumage and elegant circuit glasses only. Draw feathers, not crow portraits. Do not copy the caption.
Composition: a spacious landscape 3-column by 2-row sheet on perfectly flat midnight #10101B. Six large isolated feather concepts with equally legible framing and separate silhouettes, each with a slender quill and organic taper. Each feather mostly rises diagonally upward, but its distinctive structural form must remain clear. No boxes, dividers, UI chrome, arrows between designs or decorative background. Simple small ivory monospace captions below, exactly the six captions listed here.
01 / SPINE: a long narrow comb-like feather built around one strong central quill/bus. Repeated stepped barbs branch off as short port paths; lemon square termination pads near a few tips. Clearly long and narrow.
02 / BRANCH: a broad asymmetric feather whose long barbs visibly branch into smaller forked barbs. A recursive tree, widening toward the upper plume. Sparse mint branch paths embedded in purple structure. Clearly spreading and tapered.
03 / MESH: a broad diamond-shaped feather, formed by interwoven stepped purple barbs with a few dark diamond gaps. Fine mint paths join across the vane in a restrained mesh; occasional lemon junctions. The open lattice and broad vane are its identity.
04 / LOOP: a gracefully curved, almost hooked feather with an internal oval negative space. Its barbs join a returning path around the open centre and back to the quill. Purple curved stepped contour, mint return circuit and a couple lemon joints. Distinct ring-like construction while still recognizably a feather.
05 / BRAID: a slender split-plume feather with two distinct interlaced strands that separate, cross, and reunite toward the point. Two paths woven into the feather anatomy, purple layered barbs, mint thin connections and lemon at two merge points. Distinct fork-and-join shape.
06 / CLUSTER: a broad irregular feather with three distinct clustered groups of short barbs attached to a common quill, like little subnetworks separated by narrow negative-space notches. A few lemon hubs inside each cluster and mint connecting stems. Distinct segmented plume.
Style: sophisticated handcrafted medium-resolution pixel art, roughly 64 by 96 logical pixels per feather enlarged with hard square edges. Much richer anatomy than a tiny web icon, yet restrained enough to belong beside the small crows. Deliberate pixel clusters and flat 6-10 shade palette. Small circuit traces, branch junctions and terminal dots are legible but never overwhelm the silhouette. One or two tiny angular AND/OR-like intersections can belong naturally to the feather structure; no large gate symbols as central badges.
Color proportions: 75% dark and muted purple family based on #28243E, 20% mint #A4EDC3, 5% lemon #EEF34B. Background #10101B. Labels #F3E9D5. Keep it muted, precise and elegant.
Avoid: neon glow, halos, soft shadows, gradients, sci-fi PCB clutter, photoreal feathers, a full crow silhouette, six repeated leaf icons, identical teardrop feathers, circuits stamped onto otherwise unchanged shapes, excessive bright yellow, extra explanatory copy.

### Feather comparison, selected refinement

Use case: style-transfer
Asset type: refined Crowbo topology-feather concept board.
Input image 1 is the EDIT TARGET: the six-feather sheet. Input image 2 is STYLE REFERENCE: use the chunky left-hand Terminal Archivist's visible pixel construction and dark background.
Keep the six different topology silhouettes, composition, approximate size and relative positions from image 1. Keep exactly six feathers and the six labels SPINE, BRANCH, MESH, LOOP, BRAID, CLUSTER, with small pixel monospace type below each.
Make ONE focused visual correction across the sheet: replace the soft, glowing, finely illustrated finish with dark, flat, chunky sprite pixel art.
The background must be perfectly uniform solid #10101B edge to edge, every gap between feathers the same exact dark midnight colour. Remove every haze, gradient, glow, shadow, halo and vignette from the original. No illuminated areas around quill tips or labels. Mint and lemon are solid pigment pixels, not light sources.
Rebuild each feather on an approximately 64 by 88 logical-pixel grid, then enlarge using hard nearest-neighbour squares. A clear consistent square pixel step about 5 to 6 output pixels. Use broad grouped barbs with visibly stepped contours, simple 2-3 tone purple planes, no tiny realistic hairs, no smooth fine texture. Preserve more structural detail than a basic 32px icon, but make it visibly simpler and less refined than the edit target. The feather bodies should be dark purple #28243E and muted purple highlights; mint #A4EDC3 is used for narrow circuit paths, sparse lemon #EEF34B for terminal squares and connection dots. Maximum about 10 flat colours across all feathers. No shading gradients.
Preserve the topology differences: SPINE long narrow bus with repeat lateral barbs; BRANCH wide tree with branching barbs; MESH broad open diamond lattice feather; LOOP curved feather with an oval hole and returning spine; BRAID split plume with two crossing returning strands; CLUSTER segmented groups of barbs attached to a common quill. Paths must belong to the feather anatomy. Keep a fully visible pointed quill for each.
The whole board should now feel restrained, crisp and at home beside a terminal crow sprite. Do not introduce any new objects, typography, cards or decoration.
